Cues and timing

2.1Cue types

AudioPlays a sound file through the OpenQ mixer, with trims, loops, slices, EQ and routing.
VideoPlays a movie or still image on a stage; its soundtrack goes through the mixer.
TextShows text on a stage, such as titles, surtitles or cue lights for performers.
GroupHolds other cues and fires them in one of four ways.
WaitTakes a set time. Use it to space out an auto-follow chain.
MemoDoes nothing. Holds notes and marks places in the show.
FadeChanges another cue's level, opacity, geometry, video effects or object-audio positions over time.
StartFires another cue.
StopStops another cue.
PausePauses another cue.
Go ToMoves the playhead to another cue.
DevampLets a looping audio slice finish its pass and play on.
NetworkSends an OSC message to other gear, such as a lighting desk.
MicPlays a live audio input, such as a microphone, through the mixer.
CameraShows a live camera or capture card on a stage.
LightPlays a cue of the lighting design, or releases its sequences (see Lighting).
ArmArms another cue, so GO and its triggers fire it again.
DisarmDisarms another cue: GO and its triggers skip it.
ResetStops another cue at once and undoes what Arm, Disarm, Target and Load cues did to it.
LoadGets another cue ready to start part way through.
TargetPoints a Fade, Start, Stop, Load… cue at a different cue.
MIDISends a MIDI message: note, control or program change, pitch bend, MIDI Show Control or SysEx.
TimecodeSends timecode (LTC on an audio output, or MTC) from a start time while it runs.

Every cue has a number (free text, like 4.2), a name, notes, a colour and an armed switch, all on the Basics tab. A disarmed cue is skipped by GO. Basics also sets the cue's pre-wait, continue mode and post-wait, and, for audio and video cues, the media file.

The Triggers tab holds the ways to fire a cue besides GO: a hotkey, a key that fires the cue directly whatever the playhead is on, a MIDI trigger and a timecode trigger.

MIDI triggers

A cue can fire when a MIDI message arrives: a note on, a program change or a control change (it fires when the value rises to 64 or more, as a button sending 127/0 does). On the Triggers tab tick Fire on, pick the kind, the number and the channel (or Any), or click Learn and send the message from the keyboard, pad or desk. The cue fires whatever the playhead is on, once per press; disarmed cues don't fire.

Messages come in on this computer's MIDI input, chosen in Settings → Control surfaces (Set up… on the card opens it). The same input can also carry control surface mappings and MTC; to run the whole list from program changes, map a program change to GO there.

Wall-clock triggers

The Wall clock card on the Triggers tab fires a cue at a time of day on this computer's clock: tick Fire at, set the hours, minutes and seconds, and pick the days it fires on (none picked = every day). The cue fires as the clock passes that time. If the clock is changed or the computer was asleep, nothing fires for the time skipped. Disarmed cues don't fire.

Cues that change other cues

  • Arm and Disarm switch the target's armed setting, as if you had ticked it on its Basics tab. A cue that runs straight after (an auto-continue) already sees the change.
  • Target changes what another cue acts on: Change the target of is the cue to change (a Fade, Start, Stop, Pause, Go To, Devamp, Arm, Load… cue), New target the cue it will act on from then on. A playlist can, for example, loop from its second track after the first time round.
  • Load gets the target ready to start part way through: its next start begins Load to time seconds in, counting its pre-wait. Sound files and movies start from that point; a target that is already running jumps there. It works once: the start after that is from the beginning again. A looping movie can't be loaded.
  • Reset stops the target (and the cues inside it) at once and puts back what Arm, Disarm, Target and Load cues changed on them since the show was opened.

Arm, Disarm and Target cues change the show like an edit, so save it afterwards if the change should stay.

MIDI cues

A MIDI cue sends one message out of a MIDI output: pick the output (or First MIDI output) and the message. Notes and control changes take a channel, a number and a value; program change a program; pitch bend a value from 0 to 16383 (8192 is the centre). MIDI Show Control sends a command (GO, STOP, RESUME, LOAD…) with a cue number and list to a device ID (127 = all devices) in a command format, so OpenQ can run a lighting desk or another show computer. SysEx sends any System Exclusive message typed in hex (F0 … F7). The bytes that will be sent are shown under the settings.

2.2Timing and continues

Group modeBehaviour

Select cues and press Ctrl+G to group them. ← and → collapse and expand a group. Groups can hold other groups. Each level is inset with a guide line, so you can see where a group ends.

2.3Fades

A Fade cue changes another cue while it runs. Choose the target cue, the duration and the curve (linear, S-curve or logarithmic), then tick what to change:

  • Level in dB, for audio and for video soundtracks.
  • Opacity, for video and text.
  • Geometry: position, scale, rotation and crop. This starts from the target's own geometry, so only the values you change will move.
  • Video effects: the target's Video FX settings are copied into the fade when you tick it; change the values to fade to. An effect switched off here fades out, and effects the target doesn't have fade in from nothing. Take the target's effects copies them again after you change the target.
  • Objects, for targets in object audio mode: the positions and spread to move the objects to, edited on the same room view.
  • Output faders, for sound: the level of each output after the routing matrix (the target's Levels tab faders). Use it to move a sound from the front speakers to the surrounds.
  • Audio effects: the target's Audio FX settings glide to the values set here. An effect switched on or off, or one of another kind, changes at the start of the fade.

Light cues can't be the target of a Fade cue: lighting fades with its own Light cues (see Lighting).

Tick Stop target for a fade-out: the target stops when the fade ends. Pausing a fade freezes it, and resuming it continues with the time that was left.

2.4Timecode

OpenQ can run a cue list from incoming SMPTE timecode, for a show locked to a video, a band's click track or a lighting desk. It reads LTC (timecode as an audio signal) on an audio input or MTC (MIDI Time Code) on a MIDI input, at 24, 25, 29.97 drop-frame or 30 fps.

Setting up a cue list

Right-click the cue list's tab and choose Timecode…. Choose what the list follows (LTC or MTC), the audio input and channel or the MIDI input it arrives on, and the frame rate. The window shows the timecode coming in, and while that list is in front the standby card shows it too. Each cue list follows its own source, so one list can follow the band's LTC while another is run by hand.

Timecode triggers

On a cue's Triggers tab, tick Fire at and type the time (hh:mm:ss:ff). When the list's timecode runs past that time the cue fires, whatever the playhead is on, and its continues run as usual. The tab shows what the list follows and what it is receiving; Set up… opens the list's timecode settings.

Only timecode running forward fires cues. When the source jumps (a locate or a rewind) or stops, nothing fires, and triggering picks up again from the new position, so rehearsing a section by rolling back the source is safe.

Sending timecode

A Timecode cue sends timecode while it runs, for a lighting desk, a video server or another show computer to follow. Choose LTC (an audio signal on one output channel of the audio device, at the level set) or MTC (out of a MIDI output), the frame rate and the start time. The code runs from the start time for the cue's duration (0 = until it is stopped); pausing the cue holds the code, and resuming it carries on.
